**A lookup that failed is `could not check`, never `not found`, and `--strict` exits 2 on it.** `resolve.get` read every HTTP error it did not retry as an answer holding nothing, so a Semantic Scholar 403, a 401, a 400 or a 500 counted that service among those a `not found` rested on. Only a 404 or 410 is now an answer; an entry some services refused and the rest answered with nothing stays `not found`, with the services that refused under `refused` and named on its report line (`Semantic Scholar could not be asked`); any other 5xx is retried like a 503, and every other refusal raises `Throttled`, which `citations resolve`, `add`, `lint` and `fetch` already treat as a refusal. The audit's own registry fetch made no distinction either: a DOI Crossref and DataCite answered 404 for and a Crossref that timed out were one `unresolved` row. That status is now two: `not found` where every registry asked answered that it has no such record, and `could not check` where any of them refused, rate-limited, timed out or returned a body that is not a record, with the registries or services that did not answer under `refused`. Under `--strict`, `could not check` exits 2 when nothing else fails, so a CI job can retry instead of blaming the citation; a disagreement or an unlisted not found exits 1 whatever else could not be checked. The `--json` report is written before the summary is printed.
