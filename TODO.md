match can conflate two papers by the same author, which has happened five times in this
project, so every one of those joins is provisional until it has an identifier.

## Rate limits are the actual difficulty

All three services throttle, and they throttle *hard* after a burst. A run of sixty lookups at
half-second intervals gets refused partway through and stays refused for a while. Symptoms:
`BLOCKED` lines, or worse, a silent stream of "nothing found" that is really "I was refused."

- **Pace at 2-5 seconds between lookups.** Slower than feels necessary. A batch of 20 taking
  two minutes is fine; a batch of 60 that trips the limiter wastes far longer.
- **Work in small batches** (`--limit 20`) with a few minutes between them.
- **If everything comes back blocked, stop and come back later.** The resolver does this on
  its own after four consecutive refusals.
- Put a real mailto in the User-Agent and in OpenAlex's `mailto` parameter. Both services
  give politer treatment for it, and it costs nothing.

## The three services, and what each is for

### Crossref — journal articles, conference papers, books with DOIs

```
https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=<title>&rows=8
https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>          # look up a known DOI
```

Best coverage for anything with a publisher DOI. Returns `message.items[]` with `title[0]`,
`author[].family`, `container-title[0]`, `issued.date-parts[0][0]`, `DOI`.

Two traps. It ranks a *secondary* version first surprisingly often — the NBER working paper
rather than the JASA article, a journal-club reprint rather than the Lancet original — so
score candidates by venue agreement, not just Crossref's order. And it sometimes misspells
authors: it has Fredricks & Relman under "Fredericks", so a correct-spelling search misses it.

### OpenAlex — books, reports, theses, preprints, anything Crossref lacks

```
https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=title.search:<title>&per-page=5&mailto=you@example.com
```

Returns `results[]` with `display_name`, `authorships[].author.display_name`,
`publication_year`, `doi`, `id`. Use it as the second try. It carries DOIs under the
`10.48550/arxiv.` prefix for most preprints, which is how a work with no publisher DOI still
gets a resolvable identifier — convert those to an arXiv id rather than storing the DOI form.

It is also the right tool for settling a disagreement between two of our bibliographies about
a year or venue, because it reports the version of record explicitly. That is how the Dai
(2021 vs 2022) and Hubinger (2019 vs 2021) disputes were settled.

### arXiv — preprints, last resort

```
https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:"<title>"&max_results=4
```

XML, not JSON. Parse `<entry>` blocks for `<title>`, `<name>`, `<id>`. Strip the trailing
version (`v3`) from the id.

## The acceptance rule — do not relax it

A match is accepted only when **all three** hold:

1. **Title similarity ≥ 0.87** on the normalized string (lowercase, alphanumeric only).
2. **The first author's surname appears** in the returned author list.
3. **The year agrees within one**, where we have a year (preprint-to-publication gap).

Where a work exists in several versions, prefer the one whose venue matches the record's
`venue` field. Score candidates as `title_similarity + 1.5 * venue_similarity`.

**If any test fails, record nothing.** A missing identifier is recoverable; a wrong one is a
citation to a different paper, and it will not be caught downstream. Institutional authors
(NRC, FDA, the Nobel committee, Open Science Collaboration) have no personal surname — match
those on issuing body plus title plus year and mark them `confidence: medium`.

## Distinguish "not found" from "was refused"

This is the failure that has bitten this repository four separate times. A service that
refuses to answer is not a service reporting that it has nothing. Code that collapses the two
will confidently delete information — one batch reported sixty findable works as unfindable
because Crossref had started rate-limiting after the first few.

`resolve.py` now raises `Throttled` rather than returning `None`, tries each service
independently so one limiter does not block the others, and only counts a record as blocked
when all three refused. Preserve that distinction in anything you add.

## Known-hard cases, and what to do with them

Some records will not resolve from an API and need a human or a web search:

- **Books.** Try the publisher page, then Open Library (`openlibrary.org`), then WorldCat.
  Record the ISBN if there is no DOI.
- **Agency reports.** Go to the issuing body: `ipcc.ch`, `fda.gov`, `nap.edu` for National
  Academies, `nobelprize.org`. Several block automated fetches, so read the search result.
- **Blog posts and forum entries.** The canonical post URL is the identifier. Transformer
  Circuits Thread, the Alignment Forum and LessWrong all have stable permalinks.
- **Pre-1999 journal articles.** Often have no DOI at all. JSTOR stable IDs work
  (`jstor.org/stable/NNNNNNN`).

Two records need an editorial decision rather than a lookup, and are flagged in the records
themselves:

- **`fdabioanalytical`** — the record's title word order matches the superseded 2001 guidance
  while the live page is the 2018 revision. Decide which is meant.
- **`campbell1963experimental`** — the 1963 text appeared as a chapter in Gage's *Handbook of
  Research on Teaching* and was reprinted as a standalone Rand McNally book. A 1963 date
  points to the chapter; the standalone printing is usually dated 1966.

One will never resolve: **`tower2026mechviews`** is our own companion paper and is not posted.
